## The levers that closed them
- **FFA0: the Perm columns are `const`.** The image loads a column value before the earlier store through `out`. mwcc only moves a load above that store when the object is const. Non-const costs 32 bytes in FFA0 and 37 in ECE0. The other lever: the packed-row arm is written row by row (load, store `>> 3`, fold into p). That arm went from 17 bytes out to exact.
- **EE60: `lerp = (A && B) ? 1 : 0;` as one ternary.** The goto and if/else forms swap the lerp flag with the cursor p (the 63 bytes). The cofactor block then takes FC5C's form: three temporaries from direct field reads. That means section 8's "six bound locals" rule for EE60 was wrong, and it is withdrawn. The goto ladder rewrites as plain nested if/else and stays EXACT.
- **F844: a block-scoped initialised accumulator.** `u32 ok2 = 0;` at the top of the 3:1 arm's `if (k & 1)` block. A function-scope variable does not work.

## Still open
- **ECE0 (14 bytes):** the new natural source uses an Out* parameter with `out->m[..]` stores, a wrapping if with the flag-2 block last, the FFA0-shaped perm tail and const columns. What is left is a node/k register swap (image node in r5, k in r4). Permuter hill 800 and anneal 2000 both stay at 14, and about 40 declaration and spelling variants are flat.
- **FC5C (8 bytes):** p and oa swapped. Permuter hill and anneal stay at 8, and so do the F844 lever and the 120 initialised declaration orders.
- **F4C8 (2 bytes):** one `adds` operand order. Hill and anneal stay at 2. const key pointers do not replace the volatile (7 without it). Every spelling that names the 64-bit product fixes the operand order but costs 25 or more bytes in register allocation.
