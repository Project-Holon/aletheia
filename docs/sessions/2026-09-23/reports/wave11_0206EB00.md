## What was proven

**0x02068808** (313 -> 168)
- The clamp bounds are early locals declared at the top: `u32 w = s->w; u32 h = s->h;`. This gives the image's `ldmib` above the `popeq`. The late `sw = s->w; sh = s->h;` after the Blit stores is still reloaded, and its bytes at +0x164/+0x168 are identical.
- The tile ternaries are written else-first: `(x <= 0) ? 0 : (u32)x >> 3`.
- The swizzle locals are declared `int sy, sx;` (sy first).
- The Blit stores follow the image's order: base, h, src, w.
- Left over: the colouring of tilesz and gw, and one `stm` that the image keeps as two `str`.

**0x02068670** (254 -> 238)
- `s->w`, `s->h` and `s->base` are read inline instead of through locals.
- All three have to change together. With only one of them inline the size breaks (416 or 404 bytes). With all three the frame and push list are right.
- Left over: the image loads w, h and base at the top and spills g to its incoming slot.

**0x02068430** (159 -> 119)
- `dst` is read first.
- The cend clamp tests the re-read sum: `if ((int)(x + p->w) >= 8)`.
- The x clamp comes before the bpp read, and the y clamp after it.
- In both row bodies the pixel counter `c` is declared first.
- The last two levers came from the anneal. I applied them by hand without the `(void)&sbpp` it had added, which turned out not to be needed.

**0x0206F4C8** (unchanged at 2)
- The remaining 2 bytes are the operand order of an unnamed 64-bit sum. mwcc canonicalises that order: every commuted or retyped spelling gives the same 2 bytes.
- Naming the product gives the right order but changes the register colouring (27 bytes).
- A full twin of func_0206F214 comes out at 484 bytes, so this function does keep its separate m/j indices.

**0x0206ECE0** (unchanged at 14)
- I tried 16 combinations of the entry_of/resolve helper spellings and 5 perm-helper shapes. Only the filed nested-helper pair reaches 14.

**0x0206FC5C** (unchanged at 8)
- About 1,100 declaration orders, plus split-assign spellings of p. None moves the p/oa swap below 8.
