The shortfall is not a predicated guard block at all. Two structural holes:

 1. +0x0048/+0x004C: the image emits `mov r6, #0` then `cmp r0, #0` then
    `popls {...}`, an UNSIGNED `ls` early-out that keeps the induction
    variable initialised ahead of the test. 2.0 emits `cmp r0, #0` then
    `popeq {...}` and sinks the `mov` after it. The note's C spells the
    guard `if (n <= 0) return;` on a u32, which is the `ls`; the extra word
    the image spends is the hoisted `mov`, and 2.0 declines to hoist it.
 2. +0x006C: the image RE-READS `ldrb r0, [sb, #9]` at the top of the loop
    before `cmp r6, r0`, where 2.0 keeps the count live from the entry load
    and compares straight away. That single dropped re-read is the 4 bytes.

Both are the pinned front end's own habits (hoist the zero, re-read the
volatile-looking byte field each iteration), so this body reads as 1.1p1 and
everything after +0x0064 is a register-numbering cascade from the two holes
rather than independent evidence. Work it at the default compiler. No other
front end was tried.

The section above diagnosed hole 2 exactly right -- "the image RE-READS

compile keeps the count live from the entry load" -- and then attributed it to
the front end. It is not the front end; it is the field. The image reads
`[sb,#9]` THREE times (0x020703A4 at entry, 0x020703CC at the loop top,
0x02070418 at the loop bottom), which is only what a C compiler emits when it
may not prove the count unchanged across the `bl func_0206E7EC` in the loop
body. `volatile` is what says so, and it buys exactly the missing four bytes:
208 -> 212, the original's size, on the unchanged body of the section above.

never an independent hole at all -- it is downstream of the same decision and
