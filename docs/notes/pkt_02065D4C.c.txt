The single residue
------------------
In all three the original allocates MINIMALLY and interleaves, while
1.1p1 -O4,p hoists and over-allocates:

  * callee-saved registers.  0x02066620 is `push {r3, lr}` (0x08402de9),
    1.1p1 needs `push {r4, r5, r6, lr}` at best and `push {r4, r5, r6, r7,
    r8, lr}` for most spellings.  0x020664CC is `push {r3, r4, r5, lr}`
    (0x38402de9) against 1.1p1's eight-register
    `push {r3, r4, r5, r6, r7, r8, sb, lr}`.  0x020663D0 is
    `push {r4, r5, r6, lr}` (0x70402de9) against `push {r4, r5, r6, r7, r8,
    lr}`.
  * the zero constant.  The original holds it in `lr` (`mov lr, #0`,
    0x00e0a0e3, at 0x02066624 and 0x020664D0) and stores lr into the three
    zero slots of the stack matrix.  1.1p1 always puts it in a callee-saved
    register (r5/r2), which is what costs the extra push.
  * pool loads.  The original re-loads g_02147B88 from a fresh
    `ldr rN, [pc, ...]` at EVERY use and recomputes i*0x18 each time;
    1.1p1 caches the base in a callee-saved register (r6/r7/sb) across the
    calls, again paying a push for it.  The 0x020663D0 fast path shows this
    most clearly: the original is load/add/store per component
    (0x02066400..0x02066428), 1.1p1 hoists all three loads first.
