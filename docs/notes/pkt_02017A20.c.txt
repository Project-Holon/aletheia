The original is fully straight-line with no index register at all: six ldrh
with immediate offsets 0,2,4,6,8,0xa and six strh storing back to `sp+8`
through a second pointer set up as `add r6, sp, #8`.  Two pointers to the same
local (`sp+8` in r0 and again in r6) means the source names the destination
twice, and `add r0, sp, #8` / `add r6, sp, #8` are two separate address
materialisations, which is not how a rolled `t[k] = g[k]` loop is built.

Only TWO spellings were actually compiled, both with the copy written as a
rolled index loop, and both produced the rolled five-instruction loop instead

original's 260 rather than the rolled loop's 24 short.  So the struct/unrolled

itself is no longer a rolled loop -- but the function is still short, and the
concrete residue is WORSE than the rolled baseline (141 versus the earlier
lane's 24), because the eight bytes come back in the wrong place and every
later label shifts again.

What this rules out: the copy block alone is not the whole gap.  The earlier
section's reading -- that the two separate `add r0, sp, #8` / `add r6, sp, #8`
materialisations mean the source names the destination twice -- is not
reproduced by any of the four spellings above, including the two that name it
through a pointer.  A pointer local gives ONE materialisation that is then
reused, not two.

The remaining hypothesis, untried: the 16 bytes are not in the copy at all but
in the `i--` back-step of the inner scan, which the original may spell as a
`goto` to the top of the outer loop rather than as a decrement, and the two
`sp+8` pointers may simply be the compiler's own CSE of an address it needs on
both sides of that branch.  That would make the copy block a consequence of the
control flow rather than its cause, and it is where the next attempt should
start.
