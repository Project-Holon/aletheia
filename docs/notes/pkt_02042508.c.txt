Not filed: the size is right (172 of 172) and the only differences are where
two instructions sit.

    ORIGINAL @ +0x18                        COMPILED @ +0x18
    ldr r1, [pc, #0x7c]   ; 0xB93           ldr r2, [r7]          ; a1[0]
    ldr r0, [pc, #0x7c]   ; g_02146A44       ldr r1, [pc, #0x7c]
    str r1, [sp]                            ldr r0, [pc, #0x7c]
    ldr r0, [r0]                            str r1, [sp]
    ldr r2, [r7]          ; a1[0]           ldr r0, [r0]
    ldrh r1, [r0]                           lsr r4, r2, #8        ; id, early
    ldr r0, [pc, #0x6c]   ; 0x7FFF          ldrh r1, [r0]
    lsr r4, r2, #8                          ldr r0, [pc, #0x6c]
    and r0, r1, r0                          ldr r3, [pc, #0x5c]
    orr r0, r0, #0x8000                     and r0, r1, r0
    ...

The original sets the stack argument up first and loads `a1[0]` immediately
before the halfword it has to mask; the compiler here hoists the `a1[0]` load
to the top of the block and takes the `>> 8` with it.  Two spellings tried
(the `id` local assigned just before the key is computed, and the key bound to
a `u16` local first) both keep the hoist.

Everything from the mask onward reproduces exactly, so this is a scheduler
decision and not a shape error: the `movs` test at +0x0c, the two pool loads
(g_02146A44 and 0x7FFF), the (u16)((x & 0x7fff) | 0x8000) idiom, the
0x00000B93 stack argument and the out-of-line non-zero arm are all correct.
