The lever the previous section asked for -- "getting dx and dy onto the
stack, which is what forces that split shift pair" -- is exactly right,
and it is spelled `volatile`.  Declaring dx, dy AND dj volatile takes the
file from 308 bytes / 255 concrete bytes out to 314 / 225, and drops the
permuter's instruction-level score from 3835 to 2550.  With that, the
whole call sequence from +0x16 onward is the original's instruction
stream; what remains wrong is the stack SLOT assignment, not the code.

  - `volatile s32 dx; volatile s32 dy;` alone: 310/328, 175 bytes out,
    asm 3835.  This is what forces `ldr r0,[sp,#4]; lsls r2,r0,#3` in
    place of the folded `lsls r0, r7, #0x13`.
  - adding `volatile u32 dj`: 314/328, 225 bytes out, asm 2550, and the
    original's `ldr r0,[sp,#0xc]` reload before each `ldr r0,[r6,r0]`
    appears.  BEST POINT.
  - `volatile u32 di` instead of dj: asm 5625, much worse -- di really
    does live in a register, as the previous section said.
  - `volatile` on `dst` as well: no change at all from the dx/dy point.

Remaining residue at the best point: the original lays dy at sp+0x00, dx
at sp+0x04, dst at sp+0x08 and dj at sp+0x0c; the compile puts dj at
sp+0x18, dx at sp+0x14, dy at sp+0x10 and dst at sp+0x00, so every slot
reference is wrong by a constant and the tail is fourteen bytes short.
Re-ordering the four declarations (both directions, and with dst moved
between them) does not move them: the compiler assigns the volatile
group its own run above the non-volatile locals regardless of source
order, which is what defeats
docs/idioms/local-declaration-order-is-stack-order.md here.
