Three attestation levers that closed parked files (wave 6, pkt_0202B7BC et al.)

All three are witness problems, not codegen problems: in each case the C was
already byte-correct and the entry was capped at MATCH because the harness could
not attest a relocation target from outside the compared range.

1. An unlabelled callee defaults to ARM, so declare it thumb in `symbols:`

An `R_ARM_ABS32` pool word carries the callee's ISA in its low bit, and the ISA
the harness encodes comes from the *declaration*: an explicit `symbols:` line
first, otherwise the `func_XXXXXXXX` name convention, otherwise ARM.  `disasm.py`
only labels a prologue that saves lr, so a leaf callee that saves nothing has no
label and no `thumb` flag anywhere, and a file that reaches it through a pool
word with no `symbols:` block silently asks for an ARM target.  The `matched`
class then refuses the EXACT entry on the ISA mismatch and the site reads
"unresolved: <addr> (arm, from name) has no witness outside ...".

Three files graduated this way with a two-line header change and no C change:

    src/pending/func_0202B42C.c   needs 0x0202B490  thumb (unlabelled leaf)
    src/pending/func_0202B7C8.c   needs 0x0202B420  thumb (four-byte setter)
    src/pending/func_0202B7E0.c   needs 0x0202C0C8  thumb (eight-byte thunk)
    src/pending/func_0202B2947C.c needs 0x02012E40  thumb (one-byte getter)

and src/pending/func_0202B704.c the same once its target was corrected (see 3).

2. Each member function of a unit belongs in the unit's `function:` header list

    * function: func_0202B7F8, func_0202B804, func_0202B810

`entry_from_source()` in tools/match.py splits that header field on commas and
writes the result into the shard's `functions:` array, and `matched_index()`
adds every name that looks like `xxx_XXXXXXXX` as an address the entry attests.
A unit whose object defines three functions but whose header names one therefore
attests one address, not three - even though its one byte comparison covered all
three sections.  src/matched/unit_0202B7F8.c was that file: its `tu` map is

    0202B7F8  func_0202B7F8
    0202B804  func_0202B804
    0202B810  func_0202B810

and src/pending/func_0202B744.c was blocked on 0x0202B804.  Naming all three in
the header attests all three, and 0x0202B804 resolved.  Eleven more units in the
range under-list their members the same way (0x0201ED04, 0x0201F0F8, 0x02020F08,
0x02026454, 0x020271F8, 0x02027710, 0x0202878C, 0x02028F18, 0x02029F60,
0x0202C318, 0x0202C558, 0x0202D5F8); a unit that lists them is strictly more
informative and costs nothing.

3. A misread pool word is worth checking against the manifest before believing

src/pending/func_0202B704.c declared `func_0202C0C0` - the thunk that sits
immediately after its real target - and the `literal` class happily resolved the
site to 0x0202C0C1 because that word does appear in four literal pools.  The byte
comparison caught it as a one-byte DIFF.  The original pool word at 0x0202B70C is
0x0202C09D, i.e. 0x0202C09C with the Thumb bit, and 0x0202C09C is an existing
EXACT entry, so the fix was to rename the declaration.  src/pending/
func_02027A08.c had the identical mistake in the other direction (0x02077B30, an
ARM address outside the image's code, instead of 0x02027B30) and is EXACT now.

A cheap way to find these: for every unresolved site, read the four original
bytes at the site and look the word up in the manifest - if the *word* names an
EXACT entry while the *declaration* names something else, the declaration is the
bug.  Two of the 60-odd parked files in this range were that case.

Two files were parked the other way round, because they claimed EXACT and did
not verify: src/matched/sub_0202BA24.c (compiled 58 bytes, 53 wrong - the real
function is a constant-bound loop the compiler always rotates, see
docs/notes/pkt_0202B9F0.c.txt) and src/matched/func_0202B7BC.c (now
src/pending/func_0202B7BC.c, MATCH on 0x0202AB5C).  Neither was in a shard, so
nothing was lost, but both would have poisoned the manifest the moment their
packet's shard was written.  src/pending/func_0202AB28.c and
src/pending/func_0202AB84.c are the harmless version of the same thing: they are
byte-correct and already matched by src/matched/sub_0202AB28.c and
sub_0202AB84.c, so they can never graduate and could be deleted.
