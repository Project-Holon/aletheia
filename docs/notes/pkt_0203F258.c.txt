Two things this C pins down and that are NOT free choices:

  - The buffer declaration order is `a; c; b; d;`, not the address order
    (a at sp+0, b at sp+8, c at sp+0x1c, d at sp+0x3c).  Declared a, b, c, d
    the compiler lays c out at sp+8 and b at sp+0x28 and the two `add rN, sp,
    #imm` setups move -- that is the tell that fixes the declaration order.
  - `*(u64 *)out = *(u64 *)a;` must be an aggregate copy: spelling it as
    `out[0] = a[0]; out[1] = a[1];` emits four ldr/str instead of the
    `ldm r5, {r2, r3}` / `stm r4, {r2, r3}` pair the original has.

WHAT THIS MEANS
---------------
The eight bytes are the allocator's choice of which of {r0, r1} colours the
first of the four tail constants, and the whole tail mirrors that one
choice.  Every value-preserving spelling of the tail, and every declaration
of the parameter and of the four buffers, leaves the choice untouched; the
permuter's 3064 compiles say the same thing from the mutation side.

The function is never called directly anywhere in the image -- its address
appears only as the pool word at 0x020056CC, i.e. it is installed as a
function pointer -- so no call site constrains the signature either, and
there is no outside evidence left to bring to bear.

Do not re-attempt by spelling the tail differently.  A genuinely new reading
would have to change what happens BEFORE the ldm/stm (the five calls and the
four stack buffers), and that part is already byte-exact.
