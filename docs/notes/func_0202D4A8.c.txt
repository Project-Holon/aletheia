The residue, 29 bytes in two independent clusters:

  (a) 2 bytes at +0x1E and +0x40.  The two computed stack locals land in
      the wrong slots: the original puts the 2-way `shiny ? 9 : 0` value
      at sp+8 and the 4-way `female/shiny` value at sp+4, and reads sp+4
      as the func_020490A8 index and sp+8 as *out_pal.  Every spelling
      tried gives the mirror.  mwccarm assigns these two slots by
      FIRST-WRITE order, not by declaration order: swapping the two
      declarations (both orders tried, with and without the other four
      locals regrouped) changes nothing, and swapping the two computation
      blocks instead simply swaps which value is read where, leaving the
      same 2 bytes wrong.  Routing the call through the other variable
      (so the 4-way value is the index) forces `idx` out of r7 onto the
      stack and costs 100+ bytes, so that is not the lever either.
  (b) the 2 bytes at +0xE8, a `b` to the immediately following
      instruction.  The original's mode==1 failure path branches to the
      mode==2 arm's `movs r4,#0` rather than cross-jumping into it.
      Writing case 1 above case 2 with an explicit fall-through, or with
      an explicit `goto` to a label inside case 2, does reproduce the
      redundant branch but simultaneously permutes r4/r5/r6 for the three
      register parameters (species/form/mode), trading 2 bytes for 17.
      The two clusters appear to be coupled through the arm ordering.

Both residues are layout artefacts of a single allocator decision; the
semantics above are believed complete.  Whoever picks this up should look
for the spelling that pins the first-write order of the two flag locals
independently of the switch arm order.
