r4/r5/r6 for live values; the compiled form gives the heap a callee-saved r5 and
pushes r7 for the loop-carried `a`.  That the original keeps the value in a
caller-saved register across a call (and is still correct, because
func_02048F60 happens not to touch r2) is not reachable from the C: eleven
source shapes were tried - the heap re-read inline in the third argument, bound
to a u32 and to a u16 local, with the builder result bound to a local first or
second, the context bound to a local pointer, the heap cast, and the two
statements in both orders - and the compiled bytes for the one-argument reading
are identical in every case.

The allocator's own view is that the value crosses the call, so it must live in
a preserved register and the frame grows by one.  If someone later finds the
spelling that makes mwccarm keep it in r2, all three siblings fall at once.
