TWO THINGS THE EARLIER PASSES HAD WRONG, both worth more than any spelling
sweep:

1.  THE SOURCE CHANNELS ARE NOT BITFIELDS.  The first pass's reconstruction
    and the sibling func_02021F64 both read the pixel through a
    `struct Rgb555` bitfield pointer, which compiles to `lsls #0x16 /
    lsrs #0x1b` pairs.  The image has `asrs r2, r0, #5` then `ands r6, #0x1f`
    -- an ARITHMETIC shift of an `int` followed by an explicit mask.  So this
    function, unlike its sibling, reads the pixel into a plain `int` and does
    the extraction by hand:

        int px = src[i];
        int sg = (px >> 5) & 0x1f;

    `src` stays `u16 *` (the load is `ldrh`, not `ldrsh`); it is the `int`
    px and the C `>>` on a signed int that produce the `asrs`.  Declaring
    `src` as `s16 *` gives the right shift but costs `ldrsh` and four bytes.

2.  THE DECLARATION ORDER OF THE FIVE EXTRACTIONS IS THE WHOLE SIZE GAP.
    All 120 orderings of `sg`, `t`, `sb`, `w`, `sr` were compiled.  Eight of
    them are size-exact at 136 and the other 112 are 138 or 140.  Every
    size-exact ordering has BOTH of `t` and `w` (the two field reads off `c`)
    ahead of ALL THREE pixel extractions; no ordering that interleaves them
    reaches 136.  The best two are

        t, w, sb, sg, sr   136 bytes, 69 out     <- the source below
        w, t, sb, sg, sr   136 bytes, 69 out

    and the worst size-exact one is 75.  This is the declaration-order
    idiom (docs/idioms/declaration-order-decides-which-register-a-local-
    gets.md) deciding a size, not just a register: with a pixel extraction
    first, the pointer `c` has to stay live across it and the reload costs
    a slot.
