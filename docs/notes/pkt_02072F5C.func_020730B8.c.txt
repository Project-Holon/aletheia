THE ONE SPELLING THAT PRODUCES THE BRANCH, AND WHAT IT COSTS

A short-circuit expression as a statement defeats the predication and gives
the original's first SIX instructions byte for byte -- including the guard
load, the cmp and `bxeq lr` at +8:

    o->p == 0 || (o->p->h34 = 1, o->p->h38 = v);

compiles to 40 bytes, and the extra four are at the tail:

  +0018 ORIG ldr r0,[r0] | OURS ldr r2,[r0]     <- third volatile read, wrong reg
  +001C ORIG strh r1,[r0,#0x38] | OURS strh r1,[r2,#0x38]
  +0020 ORIG bx lr       | OURS ldr r0,[r0]     <- a FOURTH volatile read
  +0024 ORIG (end)       | OURS bx lr

The fourth read is the `||` result being materialised: it keeps `o` live to
the end, and because r0 is still live the third read is allocated r2 where the
original used r0.  Identical output from `if (o->p == 0 || (...)) return;`,
from the `&&` spelling, from `(void)(o->p && (...))` and from the comma
variant -- every one of them pays the extra load.  Making the last operand of
the comma a constant (`..., 0`) folds the whole thing back to the predicated
32-byte form.
