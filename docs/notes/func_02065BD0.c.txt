func_02065BD0: a DIFF near miss, 144 of 168 bytes, one zero-register decision
===============================================================================

Blocker of src/pending/func_0204CB44.c, which after this wave has four of its
six relocation sites resolved and needs only this address and 0x0204CC6C.  It
is not an attestation problem: with the function EXACT, every site in
func_0204CB44 except func_0204CC6C would resolve `matched`, and func_0204CB44
is in turn the sole blocker of func_0204C944 -> func_0204BB8C -> sub_0204A600.

Semantics, confidently pinned down by the byte comparison:

Resets the cell-actor / OAM bank at 0x02147B88 (u16 count at +0x00, u16 at
+0x02, pointer at +0x08, u32 at +0x0c): attaches the object, writes 0xFFFE to
the halfword at 0x020A81F8, blanks the 0x10-byte tail of each live 0x20-byte
record at 0x02148318, zeroes the count pair, clears the 0x100-byte table at
0x02147C18, writes the two GX matrix command registers MTX_PUSH (0x04000444)
and MTX_IDENTITY (0x04000454), and calls func_02064E3C.  All three globals
resolve `literal` and both helpers resolve `label`.

What was solved
---------------

Everything up to the loop exit, 92 of 168 bytes, is byte-exact:

1. The loop must be a guarded do-while with the cursor assigned inside the
   guard, not a `for` with the cursor assigned above it.  Written as a `for`,
   the cursor's pool word is hoisted above the `cmp`/`ble`; written inside the
   `if`, it lands in the preheader where the original has it, next to the
   hoisted -1 and 0x10.

2. The guard's compare is signed (`ble`, not `beq`), which a u16 count and a
   literal `> 0` cannot give: the guard has to be spelled against the counter,
   `i = 0; if (i < g.count)`, so that the promotion to int survives.

3. The record walk is a cursor (`p++`, giving `add r8, r8, #0x20`), not
   `g_02148318[i]`, which strength-reduces to `add r1, r7, r4, lsl #5`.

The residue: one constant, one register
---------------------------------------

The last 24 bytes differ, and it is a single allocation decision.  The
original materialises zero twice, once into r0 before the fill call and once
into r1 after it; the compile puts it in r4 (free after the loop, already
pushed) and keeps it live across the call, paying a `mov r0, r4` for the
argument:

  original                       compiled
  ldr r3, [pc, #0x2c]            ldr r3, [pc, #0x2c]
  mov r0, #0                     mov r4, #0
  strh r0, [r3]                  strh r4, [r3]
  ldr r1, [pc, #0x30]            ldr r1, [pc, #0x30]
  mov r2, #0x100                 mov r0, r4
  strh r0, [r3, #2]              mov r2, #0x100
  bl  #0x2082ac0                 strh r4, [r3, #2]
  ldr r0, [pc, #0x24]            bl  #0x2082ac0
  mov r1, #0                     ldr r0, [pc, #0x20]
  str r1, [r0]                   str r4, [r0]
  str r1, [r0, #0x10]            str r4, [r0, #0x10]

The tail was then isolated into a probe function of its own (just the two
halfword clears, the fill call, the two register writes and the trailing
call) and mwccarm pushes r4 *solely* to hold the zero across the call even
when nothing else in the function needs a callee-saved register.  That is the
policy, and no spelling of the tail changes it.  Tried, all giving the same
`mov r4, #0`:

  * the two register writes as `*(volatile u32 *)0x04000444 = 0` twice, as
    `reg[0]` / `reg[4]` off a local pointer, as members of a volatile
    `struct { u32 push, pop, store, restore, identity; }` at that address,
    and as two `static inline` helpers in the SDK's G3_PushMtx / G3_Identity
    shape;
  * the stored value as `(void *)0` through a `void * volatile *`, as
    `0.0f` through a `volatile float *` (the float constant is unified with
    the integer zero anyway), and as `k - k` off an unused parameter;
  * the fill value as a `const void *` NULL against a pointer-typed
    prototype, and as `g_02147B88.count` read back after being stored;
  * the halfword clears written through a `u16` local holding zero;
  * the clears moved after the fill call (worse: the call then leads and the
    clears follow it);
  * the trailing call given the register base and the zero as arguments, on
    the theory that the two words are an argument pair rather than leftovers
    (func_02064E3C overwrites r0 and r1 immediately, so they are leftovers,
    and the shape still allocates r4).

Next agent: the open question is what makes mwccarm treat the pre-call zero
and the post-call zero as two values rather than one.  It is not reachable
from the tail's own statements, all of which are pinned by the byte
comparison, and it is not a constant-CSE effect either, since a float zero
and an integer zero are unified too.  The lever, if there is one, is
something that keeps r4 occupied past the loop, or a tail whose first zero is
genuinely dead before the call.
