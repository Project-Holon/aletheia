1. The 0x0207524C/0x02075274/0x020752A4/0x020752D4 quartet, and why they are
   not one unit

All four are the same forwarding shape -- `func_020751F4(a, c, d);` then one or
two more calls on (a, a + b) -- and all four reproduce byte for byte. The pair
that closes is 0x020752A4/0x020752D4, and it closes *only because it is two
files*: 0x0208A424 is reached from exactly two sites in the whole image,
0x020752CC in 0x020752A4 and 0x020752EC in 0x020752D4. Fold the two into one
unit and both sites are inside the compared range, so the site has no witness
left and the unit caps at MATCH; kept as two files, each is the other's
witness. `--suggest-split` says the same thing, but the two-file shape is the
answer and it costs nothing.

The other two members are blocked one level deeper: 0x020896AC (called only
from 0x0207526C) and 0x020896CC / 0x0208A38C (called only from 0x02075294 and
0x0207529C) have exactly one caller each, and it is the member that needs them,
so no cut helps and they are parked. 0x020751F4, the callee they share, is
witnessed from outside (0x02073298, 0x02073DCC, 0x020758E4, 0x02075BBC).
