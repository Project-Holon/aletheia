THE THREE LEVERS THAT MATTER (each measured, each worth a lot)

(a) `o` IS A `volatile struct Obj *`.  The image re-reads `[r0, #0x20]` before
    EVERY use (31 times) and re-reads a field it has just written
    (`str r3,[r0,#0x3c]` then `ldr r2,[r0,#0x3c]` at +0x74/+0x78).  A plain
    struct pointer lets 1.1p1 forward the incoming r1 into every use and gives
    580 bytes / 480 out.  Making only `f20` volatile HOISTS all the reloads to
    the top (668 bytes / 537 out).  Qualifying the whole pointer volatile is
    the only spelling that reloads once, in place, per statement: 716 / 310.
    An offset-macro spelling (`*(s32 *)((u8 *)o + 0x24)`) reloads the INNER
    `cfg->f00` per use but still forwards r1 for `[r0,#0x20]`: 592 / 490.

(b) `pos` IS ALSO VOLATILE (`const volatile struct Vec3 *`).  Without it the
    three pos loads are hoisted into one run at the top; with it they
    interleave the way the image does (+0x14, +0x34, +0x54), and the frame
    drops from `push {r3, r4, r5, lr}` to the image's `push {r4, r5, r6, lr}`.
    712 / 209.  Then writing the three adds as `cfg_chain + pos->k` rather
    than `pos->k + cfg_chain` takes it to 712 / 185.

(c) THE 2-BIT FIELD AT BIT 0 NEEDS A SIGNED LOCAL PLUS A CAST.  Every other
    extraction in the function survives as a shift pair, but bits[1:0] --
    `lsl r1, r1, #0x1e` / `lsr r1, r1, #0x1e` at +0x214/+0x218 -- folds to
    `and r1, r1, #3` under every direct spelling, losing 4 bytes and shifting
    the whole rest of the `if` body.  Measured:
      (v << 30) >> 30 inline                     -> and  (704 B)
      u32 t = v << 30; ... t >> 30               -> and  (708 B / 197 out)
      (u32)((v << 30) >> 30) with u32 v          -> and  (708 B / 197 out)
      s32 t = v; (u32)((t << 30) >> 30)          -> PAIR (708 B / 72 out)
      s32 t = v; s32 u = t << 30; (u32)u >> 30   -> PAIR (712 B / 78 out)  <-- use this
    The last form is the one that also keeps `lsr` rather than `asr`.
    Binding the guard's `o->f20` to a local `c` and using that same `c` for
    the FIRST body statement only (later statements re-read `o->f20`) is what
    reproduces the image's reuse of r2 across the `beq` at +0x204/+0x208.
    Reading the 0x50/0x52 pair through two u16 temps (both loads, then both
    stores) is worth a further 5 bytes.

THE "arm7tdmi EPILOGUE VERSUS v5TE smulbb CONFLICT" ABOVE DOES NOT EXIST.
It is an artefact of measuring only `1.1p1`.  The whole of
0x0205429C..0x020584E0 is pinned to `../1.2/sp2` (docs/FRONTEND-PINS.md,
"the 3D rig unit"), and 1.2 at `-proc arm946e` emits BOTH horns at once:
the ARMv4T `pop {r4, r5, r6, lr}` / `bx lr` return AND the four `smulbb`.

The note's own source, unchanged except for the compiler line, measures:

    1.1p1      -proc arm946e    708 bytes,  66 concrete out   (this note)
    1.1p1      -proc arm7tdmi   712 bytes,  73 concrete out   (this note)
    ../1.2/sp2 -proc arm946e    716 bytes,  17 concrete out   <- SIZE-EXACT

Three spellings close the remaining 17.  The first was found by
`tools/permuter.py --mode hill --budget 500`, the other two by
`--mode anneal --budget 1500 --focus pos,f28,f2c,f30,cfg`, EXACT at compile
321:

  1. `volatile struct Res0 *r` for the cached descriptor pointer around the
     +0x50/+0x52/+0x54 halfword copies.  Without `volatile` the two `ldrh`
     for f1c and f1e swap registers (4 instructions).  This is the note's own
     "the two halfwords must be loaded into locals before either is stored"
     finding, but the local-pair alone is not enough: the POINTER has to be
     volatile too, or mwcc re-associates the pair.
  2. `const struct Vec3 *pos`, NOT `const volatile struct Vec3 *pos`.  The
     note's source qualifies it volatile; with volatile, the three position
     adds and the whole descriptor chain that feeds them are 10 instructions
     of register naming out (r1<->r4, r5<->r1, r6<->r5).
  3. `o->f28 = pos->x + o->f20->f00->f04;` -- POSITION FIRST.  The note's
     source writes the field first.  Field-first costs the same 10
     instructions in the other direction; this is
     docs/idioms/commutative-operand-order-names-the-temporary.md, and the
     image's `add r1, r5, r1` with r5 = pos->x on the left names it.

Everything else is the note's source and is unchanged and correct: the
`volatile struct Obj *o`, the every-time re-read of `o->f20->f00` for each
copied field, `(s16)(x * -1)` for the four sign flips (which is what gives
`mvn r1, #0` + `smulbb r1, r2, r1` and NOT `rsb` -- measured this wave, the
alternatives `* (u16)0xffff` (a pooled word and a 32-bit `mul`), `* (s16)-1`,
`* ~0`, a `s16 neg = -1` local, a file-scope `static const s16`, and
`-1 * (s32)x` all fail), and the bitfield spellings of the two 2-bit
exponents and their sign bits.
