1.  THE GUARD'S EARLY RETURN LIVES AT THE END OF THE FUNCTION (410 -> 236).
    The original opens

        ldrsb r1, [r5, #0x136] ; cmp r1, #0 ; beq body ; b +0x2016b74

    and 0x02016b74 is past the last epilogue.  Written as an ordinary
    early return the compiler emits the decrement inline and shifts the
    whole body down by four bytes.  It must be a `goto` to a block placed
    after the normal return:

        if (*(s8 *)(a1 + 0x136) != 0) goto dec;
        ...
        done: *(s8 *)(a1 + 0x136) = *(s8 *)(a1 + 0x137); return;
        dec:  *(s8 *)(a1 + 0x136) = *(s8 *)(a1 + 0x136) - 1;

    through-goto.md seen from the other side: here the goto is what moves
    the rare arm out of line, not what merges two exits.

    extraction, so the shift-pair spelling is safe here (contrast the
    one-bit case in docs/notes/pkt_02014C08.c.txt, where it is not).

3.  THE NARROWING BELONGS TO THE CALLEE'S PARAMETER, NOT THE CALL SITE
    (182 -> 15).  The third argument is a signed halving of the +0xdc
    size, which the original emits as

        lsrs r3, r2, #0x1f ; adds r3, r2, r3 ; lsls r2, r3, #0xf ; lsrs r2, r2, #0x10

    i.e. `(x + (x >> 31)) << 15 >> 16` -- a signed `/2` and ONE narrowing
    to 16 bits.  Writing `(u32)(u16)((s32)x / 2)` at the call site adds a
    second `lsls/lsrs` pair and costs 4 bytes plus the whole tail's
    alignment; declaring the parameter `u16` and passing the plain
    `(s32)x / 2` produces exactly the original's four instructions.  The
    prototype that reproduces it is

        void func_02021F64(void *a, void *b, u16 c, u8 d, u16 e);

    This is the same lesson docs/families/mcss.h records for the heap
    word, pointing the other way: a plain field wants its narrowing at
    the use, a call argument wants it in the callee's declared type.

4.  `step` MUST BE DECLARED BEFORE `cur` AND `tgt` (15 -> 4).
    docs/idioms/declaration-order-decides-which-register-a-local-gets.md.
    The interpolator tail needs `tgt` in r3 and the step's sign test's
    operand in r2; with the locals declared

        struct Obj *p; u32 flag; void *ret; s8 cur; s8 tgt; s32 step;

    the allocator hands tgt r2 and the step r3, and eleven bytes across
    six compares/stores come out swapped.  Moving `s32 step;` ahead of
    the two `s8`s -- i.e.

        struct Obj *p; u32 flag; void *ret; s32 step; s8 cur; s8 tgt;

    -- puts them the right way round and every one of those eleven bytes
    closes.  The three other orders tried (tgt before cur; the three
    tail locals first; p/tgt/cur/step/flag/ret) all stay at 15.
