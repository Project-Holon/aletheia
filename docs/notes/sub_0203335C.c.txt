LEVERS THAT LANDED (each is required; dropping any one costs bytes):

  * `do { } while (i < n)` rather than `for`/`while`.  The `while` form emits
    a second `cmp n, #0` / `popls` guard on top of the original's
    flag-reusing `adds ip, r0, #2` / `popeq` pair and is 164 bytes, 8 over.
  * `n` must be a DECLARATION INITIALISER evaluated before the zeroing of
    w/sh/i, and the `if (n == 0) return;` must come after all four.  The
    original's `mov r2,#0 / mov r3,r2 / mov lr,r2 / adds ip,r0,#2 / popeq`
    is exactly that order.  Initialising the loop variables after the guard
    instead (the natural spelling) puts the `adds`/`popeq` first and costs
    20 more bytes (52 against 32).
  * All loop variables u32, not int: the original's `subhs`/`addhs` are the
    UNSIGNED conditionals; an int gives `subge`/`addge`.  (Same finding as
    docs/notes/pkt_02033410.c.txt, where it was worth 38 bytes.)
  * The clear mask is the 64-bit complement `(3 << sh) ^ -1`, never
    `~(3 << sh)` -- see docs/idioms/
    a-64-bit-complement-is-eors-with-minus-one-not-mvns.md and the header of
    src/matched/sub_0203331C.c.
  * A COPY of the bound, `n_ = n;`, declared between `sh` and `i`, with the
    loop testing `n_` and the guard testing `n`.  This is the lever that
    closed the sibling func_02033410 outright; here it is worth 11 bytes
    (32 -> 21) but not the rest.
  * `tmp = 3 << sh;` lifted out of the `if`, and the `(u32)` cast on the
    first arm's comparison.  Both came out of the permuter search and are
    worth 6 more bytes; neither is semantically meaningful.
