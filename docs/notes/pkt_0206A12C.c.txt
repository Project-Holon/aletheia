The whole packet is the flags object at 0x02148B4C (a 64-word array whose word

Nine of the twelve open functions turn on one allocator behaviour:

  mwccarm keeps a *global's address* live across a call in a callee-saved
  register (here r4, hence a `push {r4,lr}` and 4 bytes the original does not
  have).  The original re-materialises the address from its pool word after
  the call instead: `ldr r0,[pc] ; ldr r1,[r0,#252]` again.  Every spelling
  tried -- array indexing `g[63]`, a `(char *)g + 252` cast, a named `u32 *f`
  local, `volatile` on the global, `volatile` on the single access, and even
  writing the address as an integer literal to defeat the relocation --
  either keeps the address in a register (64 bytes against 68) or folds the
  second access into the first pool word (`ldr r0,[r3,#28]`), which is the
  literal-address variant and loses the second ABS32 outright.
  The one case that comes out framed but frameless (func_0206A9DC) still
  assigns the copy destination to lr, saving lr and costing 4 bytes.

Two further residues worth naming:
  * func_0206A854: the original's second call argument is the post-increment
    pointer (`ldr r0,[r1],#4`, so r1 arrives as &g[1]) and bits 0 and 1 are
    cleared with two separate `bic`s, not one `bic #3`.
  * func_0206A17C: the original loads the word at +44 *after* the strh and
    reuses r2 (the halfword temp) for it; every order tried hoists the load

func_0206A884 is a four-call registration block (0x1610/0x11, 0x19/0xC,
0x10/0x1 with the address of a one-word local, 0x15/0x20) ending in
`g_02148B4C[63] = (g_02148B4C[63] & ~1) | 2`.  All four object addresses

What it cost was where the one-word local is declared.  Written at the top of
the block, the compiler puts `mov r3, #2` / `str r3, [sp]` before the *first*
call; the original has both immediately before the third call, i.e. the
declaration sat at its first use.  This front end refuses that as a mixed
declaration after a statement even at `-lang c99` ("expression syntax error"),
so the spelling that works is a nested block: `{ int v = 2; f(0x10, &v, 1); }`.
Same frame, same instructions, one schedule.
