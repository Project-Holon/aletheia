pkt_020113F0 (ica_anime.c) - the two near misses left open
==========================================================

Nine of this packet's fourteen functions are filed EXACT (0x02011410,
0x020114D0, 0x02011558, 0x02011590, 0x020115C0, 0x020115EC and the two
four-byte getters, 0x02011610, 0x02011638, plus the twelve-byte 0x02011660
that carries no label).  Two are left, and neither is mistranslated.

1. func_02011400 (0x02011400, 12 bytes) - one instruction's scheduling

   02011400  lsls r2, r0, #2
   02011402  adds r2, r0, r2        ; row * 5
   02011404  ldr  r0, pool          ; 0x0209DF10
   02011406  adds r0, r0, r2
   02011408  ldrb r0, [r1, r0]
   0201140A  bx   lr

Three spellings get two of the four arithmetic instructions right and the
fourth wrong, and they split the ways the idiom list predicts:

  * `p[row * 5 + (u32)g_0209DF10]`      -> schedule right, the add's operands
                                           the wrong way round (`adds r0, r2, r0`)
                                           - that is what `off + table` means to
                                           the front end, the left operand is
                                           the destination;
  * `p[(u32)g_0209DF10 + row * 5]`      -> the add right, but the constant is
    and the same with a local for the      loaded one slot earlier, above the
    table or for the offset                `adds r2, r0, r2`.

The original wants the C source order "offset first" (so the multiply is
issued first) with the machine add in the table-first order, which is
unreachable from either spelling.  This is the same class as the
"a materialised shift temporary is not reachable from C" idiom: one
scheduling slot below the source text.

The function is also capped independently: 0x0209DF10 is a flat byte table
with exactly one pool word in the 3.9 MB image, this one, so the `literal`
class has no witness and no cut helps.  It is the same defect as its
neighbours src/pending/func_020113F0.c (table 0x0209DF24) and the parked
0x02011400 in this note: private tables, not mistranslations.

2. func_02011688 (0x02011688, 54 bytes) - a guard's compare and a copy

The modulo is right: `u32 idx = v % period;` produces the single call to
_u32_div_f (0x0209C2B0) and reads the remainder out of r1.  What does not
reproduce is the two instructions after it:

   02011698  ldr  r0, [r5, #0x18]   ; f18
   0201169A  adds r2, r1, #0        ; the remainder settles into r2 HERE
   0201169C  cmp  r0, r6
   0201169E  bne  ret0

Every spelling tried (sixteen) keeps the remainder in r1 and issues the
`cmp` first, so the whole tail is two bytes out of phase, and the twelve-byte
copy then differs too: the original copies three words one at a time
(`ldr r0,[r1,r3]` / `adds r1,r1,r3` / three `ldr`/`str` pairs) where a
`*dst = table[idx]` struct assignment is recognised as a <= 32-byte aggregate
and becomes an ldm/stm pair plus one word.  So the source copied fields, not
the struct - but every field-wise spelling tried (a `u32 *`, a `Rec12 *`, a
byte pointer with the multiply by 12 in the source) also reorders the guard.

Spellings tried, all DIFF: two locals with the frame named (4 bytes off),
one local (9), the pointer local declared first (43), the guard written
positively (25), the structure assignment (24), the byte pointer with
`idx * 12` (16), the fields read from `((Rec12 *)p->f1c)[idx]` (37).
The residue is at most a four-byte register choice on the guard - worth one
more look by whoever next holds this packet, with the two hints that the
remainder is moved into its register BEFORE the compare and that the copy is
three separate word loads.
