Not filed: the size is exactly right (236 of 236) and the whole of both four-
slot loops, the failure report, the +0x90 index store and the 0x021469F0
reload after every call reproduce.

The residue is that the six zero-byte stores address the stack frame through
r8 rather than directly:

    ORIGINAL                                 COMPILED
    add r8, sp, #0                           mov r4, #0
    mov r4, #0                               ldr r6, [pc, #0x84]
    ldr r6, [pc, #0x80]                      add r8, sp, #0
    strb r4, [r8]                            strb r4, [sp]
    strb r4, [r8, #1]                        strb r4, [sp, #1]
    ...                                      ...

Both forms are the same six stores; the original materialises the buffer
address once, before the zeroing, and keeps it in r8 for the second pass's
`mov r0, r8`, while the compiler here addresses sp directly and only computes
r8 where the call needs it.  Tried: writing the stores through `u8 buf[]`
directly with sp addressing (same, and the r8 computation sinks further), and
binding `u8 *p = buf;` immediately before the zeroing -- the compiler folds the
pointer back to sp for the stores and sinks the address computation, giving
the identical bytes as the direct form.

The six stores must be six separate byte assignments and `buf` must be eight
bytes: a `memset` or a zero initialiser collapses them into a word plus a
halfword, and a six-byte frame loses the `add sp, sp, #8` pairing.
