68 bytes compiled against the original's 58, and the only differences are
the register the two loaded values land in and the push set:

    original  push {r4, r5, r6, lr}   ldr r6, [r5, r4]   ... ldr r6, [r5, r0]
    compiled  push {r3, r4, r5, lr}   ldr r1, [r5, r4]   ... ldr r1, [r5, r0]

The original reuses r6 for both temporaries (the count and then the index)
and keeps 0x1558 in r4; the compiler here picks the scratch r1 for both and
leaves r3 as the alignment push.  Eleven spellings were tried: pointer
arithmetic on `obj + 0x1558` (the compiler folds it into r4+base), the
`p[-1]` form, `(u8)`/`u32`/`char` temporaries, a single reused local for both
loads, an assignment inside the guard, and volatile-qualified loads; each
either keeps the r1 choice or makes the frame worse.  If both loads share a
register, the source probably reused one variable for both -- but no
spelling of that reaches r6 either.  Four of the six differing bytes are the
push/pop pair, so the function is otherwise right.

2. 0x0200EC2C, 108 bytes, DIFF: 76 bytes, scheduling and masks
-------------------------------------------------------------
src/pending/func_0200EC2C.c.  The C shape is not in doubt: a 0x3c-byte
source record indexed by `idx` (the multiply by 0x3c is in the first four
instructions) whose fields move into a destination object, two of them
through memcpy (func_02045994, arm, attested label x2) and four halfwords
at the end through a rotated loop.  What does not line up:

  * the destination's byte at +0x13 is written twice, low six bits then bits
    6-7, from the source byte at +3 -- a `u8 a:6; u8 b:2;` pair on both
    sides is the natural reading, and every spelling tried (bitfield on both
    sides, bitfield on the destination only, `& 0x3f` / `>> 6` with a u8
    local, the two shifted forms with casts) differs in the *mask*
    instruction: the original truncates to six bits with the shift pair
    `lsls #26 / lsrs #26` and then masks the insert with a register holding
    0x3f, where every attempt here emits a single `ands #0x3f`;
  * the destination field load at +0x13 is scheduled early (offset +0x0c)
    against the original's +0x1e, which shifts every following byte.

Thumb has no immediate AND, so both spellings are two instructions and the
choice is the front end's; this is the `a-materialised-shift-temporary-is-
not-reachable-from-c-or-from-flags.md` class of residue rather than a source
question.
