func_020422D4(kind, &w, &h) is the 140-byte six-case dispatcher at 0x020422D4
that writes a tile WIDTH and HEIGHT byte through two out-pointers; here they
are two adjacent stack bytes, `add r1, sp, #0x1d` and `add r2, sp, #0x1c`, so
the width is the HIGHER address of the pair. Those two bytes are then the
clipping bounds for every row and column.

`mode` selects between two whole loop nests (`ldrb r0, [sp, #0x60]` /
`cmp r0, #0` / `bne 0x2042c40`):

  mode == 0   straight halfword copy. Per row i and column j, copy
              src[sx + sw * (u8)(sy + i)][j] to the destination cell, guarded
              by `(u8)(dy + i) < th && (u8)(sy + i) < sh` on the row and
              `(u8)(dx + j) < tw && (u8)(sx + j) < sw` on the column.
  mode != 0   the indirect form: each cell's index goes through
              func_02040F84((u8)(sx + j), (u8)(sy + i), sw, sh) -- the
              268-byte quadrant router at 0x02040F84 -- and the halfword is
              read from `src[that]`.

THREE TELLS SETTLED, and they are what takes the body from 528 to the exact
520:

  * sx and sy are u8 PARAMETERS, not ints. The image reads them with `ldrb`
    straight out of the outgoing argument area (`ldrb lr, [sp, #0x54]` and
    `ldrb fp, [sp, #0x50]` at 0x02042B94/0x02042B98, and again at
    0x02042C58/0x02042C60 in the second nest). Declared `s32` the body is
    528 bytes.
  * sw and sh are UNSIGNED. Every bound comparison in the image is `lo`/`hs`,
    never `lt`/`ge` (`cmplo r1, r6` at 0x02042BC0, `cmplo r1, r7` at
    0x02042C04); signed types give `bge` and change the polarity of the whole
    guard chain.
  * `mode` is a u8 as well (`ldrb r0, [sp, #0x60]`).
  * Every loop counter wraps at 8 bits: `add r1, r5, #1` / `and r5, r1, #0xff`
    (0x02042C14) and `add r0, r4, #1` / `and r4, r0, #0xff` (0x02042C24). Both
    counters must be written `i = (u8)(i + 1)`, not `i++` on a u8 local, or
    the mask moves into the compare.
  * The two row/column guards are ONE conjunction each, not a chain: the image
    predicates the second test (`addlo r2, lr, r4` / `andlo r1, r2, #0xff` /
    `cmplo r1, r6`) and branches once, which only happens with `&&`.

THE RESIDUE, and it is a frame/allocation difference, not a shape one. The
image is `push {r3, r4, r5, r6, r7, r8, sb, sl, fp, lr}` -- TEN registers, the
r3 being the odd-alignment pad -- plus `sub sp, sp, #0x20`; every spelling
measured gets NINE registers and `#0x1c`, one stack word less. Inside the
first nest the image recomputes the destination row base per iteration,
`ldr r1, [sp, #0x14]` (the spilled d->f00) / `add r1, r1, sl, lsl #1` /
`add r0, r1, r0, lsl #1` at 0x02042BE0..0x02042BEC, where 1.1p1 hoists
`buf + dx * 2` out of the whole loop into fp. In the SECOND nest the image
does hoist it (`mov r0, r4` / `add r0, r0, sl, lsl #1` / `str r0, [sp, #0x18]`
at 0x02042C68..0x02042C70), so the two nests genuinely differ and the C must
make them differ.
