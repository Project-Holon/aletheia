Three findings are load-bearing and cost real time to establish:

  - the two u8 arguments land at +6 and +7, NOT +8 and +9.  The record's
    first three fields are u32/u16/u8/u8, so a struct that starts u32,u32
    shifts every later field by 4 and turns a clean match into a 60-byte
    scatter that looks like a codegen problem.  The tell is `strb r6,
    [r0,#6]`.
  - func_02012BB0 RETURNS A VALUE and that value is what lands at +0x10.
    Declaring it `void` and writing a separate `infowin_ptr->f10 = 0;`
    after the if/else costs 4 bytes and 40 differing: the original's two
    arms converge on one `str r0,[r1,#0x10]` that stores whatever r0
    holds -- the call's return value on the non-null path, and the zero
    left over from the `f0c = 0` store on the null path.  Writing the
    store into BOTH arms (`f10 = func_02012BB0(a2);` and `f10 = 0;`) is
    what lets the compiler cross-jump them onto the single site; see
    docs/idioms/a-shared-exit-block-is-written-out-in-each-arm.md.
  - the global at 0x0202693C is re-read from memory before every single
    field access -- eleven `ldr r0,[r4]` in 140 bytes.  This falls out for
    free from a plain (non-volatile) global pointer, because every
    intervening callee is an extern that may alias it.  Do NOT reach for
    `volatile` here: qualifying the pointer volatile is strictly worse
    (168 bytes, 132 differing), because it also blocks the reuse of r4 as
    the pool base.

Where the diff lives: the entry block schedules differently.  The original
is  `str r3,[sp,#4]` / `ldr r0,[pc]` / `adds r7,r1` / `str r0,[sp]` /
`adds r5,r2` / `ldr r0,[sp,#4]`  -- it spills the heap argument to sp[4]
and RELOADS it into r0 for the call, even though r3 still holds it.  mwcc
emits `ldr r0,[pc]` / `str r3,[sp,#4]` / `str r0,[sp]` / `adds r0,r3,#0`
/ `adds r7,r1` / `adds r5,r2`, reusing r3 directly and saving the reload.
That reload is the 4-byte size gap; the rest of the 20 is the reordering
around it.

Tried and rejected:
  - forcing the reload by qualifying the heap parameter `volatile`, and by
    binding it to a `volatile u32` local before the call: both much worse
    (164 bytes, 144 differing).  The volatile makes the spill happen but
    also forces a reload at the func_020251FC site, where the original
    already has one naturally.
  - a plain `u32 h = heap;` alias local: no change (160/20).
  - the two u8 parameters widened to u32 with (u8) casts at the store
    sites: no change (160/20), so the parameter types are not the lever.
  - reordering the two `strb` stores and the func_020251FC call: no
