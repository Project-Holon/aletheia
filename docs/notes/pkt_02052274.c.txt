---- what fixed the size and the frame ----

Three levers, in the order they mattered:

1. BOTH cursors are initialised BEFORE the `n == 0` guard.  Writing
   `i = 0; j = 0; if (n == 0) return;` is what produces the original's
   `mov r6, #0` at +0008 ahead of `movs r8, r2`, the nine-register
   `push {r3, r4, r5, r6, r7, r8, sb, sl, lr}` and the `sub sp, sp, #4`
   pad.  With the guard first, the compiler drops to eight callee-saved
   registers, needs no pad, and lands at 92 bytes.  This single change is
   worth 20 bytes of frame.

2. The scratch word is RESET INSIDE the loop from a separate zero-valued
   local (`z`), not initialised once outside.  `scratch = z;` at the top of
   the body gives the original's `mov r5, r6` / `str r5, [sp]` pair; a
   single `scratch = 0;` before the loop loses the copy and the register.

3. `i++` sits BEFORE the `scratch == 1` early return, not after it.  The
   original's `add r6, r6, #1` at +0054 is ahead of the `addeq sp, sp, #4` /
   `popeq` epilogue pair; putting the increment after the guard emits it
   below them.  Worth 12 bytes.

---- the 8-byte residue ----

  * +0024    mov r5, r6            | add r5, sp, #0
  * +0028    add r4, sp, #0        | mov r4, r6
  * +002C    mov r2, r4            | mov r2, r5
  * +0038    str r5, [sp]          | str r4, [sp]

Two values are materialised at the same point - the scratch POINTER
(`add rD, sp, #0`) and the scratch's zero SEED (`mov rD, r6`, copied from
the already-zero i) - and the two compiles disagree only about which gets
r4 and which gets r5.  Every instruction is otherwise identical, in order,
and the r2 argument setup and the `str` follow the swap consistently.  The
image gives the SEED the lower register (r4 <- pointer, r5 <- seed is the
compile; the image is r5 <- seed... read the columns: image has
`mov r5, r6` first, then `add r4, sp, #0`, i.e. the seed is materialised
FIRST and takes r5 while the pointer takes r4; the compile materialises the
pointer first into r5).  So the tell is materialisation ORDER at equal
priority, and the C does not appear to reach it.

Ten spellings measured, all 8 bytes or worse:

  - declaration order of `z` and `p`: all six permutations of
    {scratch, z, p, i, j} that keep i/j adjacent - 8, 8, 8, 21, 21, 40
  - initialisation order `z = i; p = &scratch;` vs `p = &scratch; z = i;`
    - 8 and 21 (so the assignment order DOES matter, and the better of the
      two is already the one that matches the image's seed-first order;
      it just does not move the register choice)
  - `z = i` vs `z = 0` - both 8
  - scratch reached through `*p` throughout instead of by name - 10
  - `scratch` declared as `u32 scratch[1]` with `p = scratch` - 8
  - subscript call arguments `&a[i * 2]`, `&b[j]` instead of pointer
    arithmetic - 8

---- not pinnable ----

No compiler probe was run, and none is authorized: the residue matches none
of the three tells in docs/FRONTEND-PINS.md.  There is no kept branch (no
guard block predicated away - the instruction COUNT is identical), no kept
constant (nothing is rematerialised that the other build forwards; both
builds materialise the same two values at the same point), and no re-read of
a just-loaded chain.  A two-register swap at equal materialisation priority
is a scheduling coin-flip, not a front-end signature.  Do not sweep it.
