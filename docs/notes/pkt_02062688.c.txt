The source recorded above (286 of 336 concrete bytes out) is superseded.  It had
the right SEMANTICS but the wrong shape in both halves, and the register residue
it reports as "7 callee-saved vs 5" is a consequence, not a cause.  Two
structural facts were read off the image and neither is in that source:

    sub-bank walk advances `add r3, r3, #0xc` and the inner one `add r8, r8, #4`,
    with a SEPARATE plain counter compared against a reloaded `ldrh`

    `mul` by 12 and pays an `lsl #16`/`lsr #16` truncation pair per iteration.
    The counters in this half are u32; only the FIRST half's two counters are
    u16 (they do carry the truncation pairs, at +0x70/+0x80 and +0x94/+0x9C).

    This is the same "re-read the optimiser deletes" lever that closed the
    cluster's sibling func_0206296C this wave -- see the new

    stands open.

THE ONE REMAINING OBSTACLE, stated precisely.  The re-load and the `ldmib`
merge cannot currently be had at the same time:

  - WITHOUT `volatile`, the two reads of +0x04 and +0x08 are folded into the
    values just stored (a9: `add r4, r1, r0` / `str r4, [r0, #4]` reuses r4),
    so there is no second load to merge and the function is four bytes short.
  - WITH `volatile` (variant a5/a8, 340 bytes), the second load DOES appear,
    correctly placed at +0x30 and correctly into r4 and r5 -- but as two
    separate `ldr`s, because the compiler may not combine volatile accesses.
    Four bytes long instead of four bytes short.

That the merge itself is reachable is proved from the other direction: writing
the three fixups as plain field stores makes the compiler emit `stmib r0,
{r4, r5}` for the +0x04/+0x08 PAIR OF STORES (variant b2).  So the peephole
fires on this exact register pair and offsets; only the volatile qualifier on
the loads blocks it.
