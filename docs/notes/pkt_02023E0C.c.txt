Three spellings were load-bearing, and two of them are worth carrying to
any future attempt on sub_02023E0C, since that function lives in the same
translation unit:

  - `p->f1c = 1;` must be the FIRST statement of the function.  The
    constant 1 has to land in r0 before the two stack arguments are
    widened; written anywhere later in the block the allocator promotes it
    to r6 and the whole entry block shifts register bank.  Measured at
    three positions: first = EXACT, third = 14 bytes wrong, second = 19.
    This is a real, cheap lever against exactly the kind of entry-block
    bank shift the note above is stuck on, and it did not appear in that
    note's list of things tried.  Worth a retry on sub_02023E0C.
  - the two arguments arriving at sp+0x10 and sp+0x14 are widened with
    `lsls #0x18` / `lsrs #0x18`, so they are INT parameters narrowed by a
    (u8) cast, not u8 parameters.  Declaring them u8 makes the compiler
    emit `ldrb` from the stack slot instead and loses 4 bytes.
  - the negation in the sign branch re-reads the field: the original is
    `ldrsh` + `subs r1,#0x13` + `muls`, four bytes more than the `rsbs`
    the compiler folds from the value it already has in a register.  A
    volatile-qualified read (`*(volatile s16 *)&p->f12 * -1`) is what
    defeats that fold; without it the function compiles 84 bytes, not 88.
    The -1 multiplier and the -1 stored to f10 in the other arm share one
    register, derived by `subs #0x13` from the 0x12 field offset already
    live for the `ldrsh`.

`/` and `%` on the same operands are two separate _s32_div_f calls, as
docs/idioms/div-and-mod-are-two-calls.md says; f14 takes the quotient from
r0 and f16 the remainder from r1.
