func_02052F58 / func_02052F90 are the interesting residue (48 bytes each,
44 compiled).  The original opens

    stmfd sp!,{lr} ; sub sp,sp,#4      (a live 4-byte frame, never addressed)
    ldr r3,[pc]  -> &g_020A8148 ; ldr r3,[r3] ; blx r3
    ldr r1,[pc]  -> 0x0000FFFF
    and r0,r0,r1 ; lsl r0,r0,#3

so the mask is a *pool word* and a real `and`, and the frame is the
signature of a 4-byte local that stayed in a register.  Every spelling tried
-- `x & 0xFFFF`, `(x & 0xFFFFu)`, `(u16)x << 3`, a `u16` local, `u32`/`int`
local masks, `x % 65536 * 8`, a ternary -- folds the mask into a single
`lsl #16 / lsr #13` pair and drops the frame (36 bytes).  Only a *64-bit*
operand forces the pool mask back (`long long x` gives the right `and` and a
44-byte function with `push {r3,lr}`), which is why the note keeps the 64-bit
shape: the remaining 4 bytes are the frame, i.e. a 32-bit local that mwccarm
refuses to fold.

That 0x0000FFFF pool word is also the cleanest test in the packet for
`(u16)` cast versus mask: the cast is a shift pair, the mask plus a 64-bit
value is a pool word.  func_02051AA0 (0x02051AA0, pkt_02051A54) shows the
same tell in the other direction: `(u16)f(...) << 3` compiles to
`lsl #16 / lsr #13` with no pool word at all.
