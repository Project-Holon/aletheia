One register of difference in the allocator, and it costs a callee-saved
register (fp vs r4), which is what then makes the rest of the function
re-allocate.  Tried, with the same flag set: the base as a named `u8 *`
local declared after `idx`; the base spelled as a literal inline at each of
its three uses; `idx` declared before the base; and `((Hdr *)0x02150FEC)->b`
(a member access at +4, which is what keeps the original's `ldr r1,[r0,#4]`
from being folded to a second pool word).  None of them moves the product off
r5.

There is one more, larger residue behind it.  The original re-loads both
0x02150FEC and 0x02153B8C from their pool words at every use site and writes
the table slot with an addressing-mode store (`str r3, [r2, r6, lsl #2]`),
which is why its arms are 8 and 6 instructions.  Every spelling tried lets
mwcc CSE the 0x02153B8C base into a register and sink the store into the
shared tail, giving 4-instruction arms and the 8-byte shortfall.  This is the
same "address of a constant survives CSE" shape that has no C lever.
