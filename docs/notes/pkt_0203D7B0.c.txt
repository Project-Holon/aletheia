This is the `ldr ip,[pc]; bx ip; .word <target>` linker veneer, the first of
the two shapes the R8 rules list for this band.  Here - unlike the 0x020911F0
family in docs/notes/pkt_020911D8.c.txt - the pointer word names a region that
IS extracted: 0x0203C8E8 (64-byte ARM function) and 0x0203C900 (52-byte ARM
function).  That makes a *call site* that targets the stub attestable through
the `veneer` class ("the stub itself, read from outside your range, is the
evidence", docs/AGENT-BRIEF.md section 5), which is why layout/function_starts
and the overlay listings carry the `blx` sites.  It does NOT make the stub rows
themselves filable: `ldr ip,[pc]` + `bx ip` with no prologue, no local and no
argument movement is not emittable from any C spelling (the compiler's own
interworking / far-branch sequences are the ones that *create* a veneer, and
they are never generated inside a single-function section - see
docs/idioms/a-four-byte-arm-function-in-a-thumb-packet-is-a-long-branch-veneer.md
and docs/idioms/a-veneer-call-is-an-ordinary-extern-at-the-veneer-address.md).

Verdict: parked, class veneer, no source filed for either row.  Reported as a
class in this lane's report rather than transcribed.  The worklist's 8-byte
size for each row is the stub alone; the pool word is what the next row's
`address:` does not cover, and the pair's real extent is the 12 bytes
0x0203D7B0..0x0203D7BC and 0x0203D7BC..0x0203D7C8 that `match.py show` prints.
