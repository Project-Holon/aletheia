What it is: a 15-bit 5:5:5 colour blend over n pixels, per channel
`out = c + ((target - c) * k) >> 4`, packed
`dst[i] = (u8)r | ((u8)g << 5) | ((u8)b << 10)`.

Two bitfield tells carry the whole body and are worth keeping:
  - `pal->v` is a 15-bit **bitfield** at +4 (lsls 17/lsrs 17 at +0x2A), read
    into an `int` local, which is why the following `>> 10` / `>> 5` are
    **asrs**.  Written as `pal->f4 & 0x7fff` the compiler instead
    materialises 0x7fff out of the literal pool (worse, and the only
    spelling that changes the byte count for a different reason).
  - `pal->k` is a 5-bit bitfield at bits 6-10 of the halfword at +2 (lsls
    21/lsrs 27 at +0x32).  Written as `(f2 & 0x7ff) >> 6` it is 8 bytes of
    shift plus an `ldr` from the pool for the mask.
The three channel blends are emitted r, b, g in that order.

The residue (all four spellings tried below compile to the same 140):

  original                    compiler
  i        r5                 r4
  g        r6                 r7
  rr       r7 (truncated)     r0
  pal      reloaded [sp+8]    held in a register
  bb<<10   r0                 r6

The original spills the palette pointer and reloads it for each of the three
field reads, and keeps `i`/`g` (not `rr`/`bb`) in the callee-saved slots
r5/r6.  Every spelling tried keeps the pointer live in a register instead and
lands the truncated channel in a scratch register, which costs
`adds r5, r0, #0` / `adds r7, r2, #0` -- the 4 bytes.

Spellings tried:
