pkt_02082F48 - hand-written ARM, not compiled C
==============================================================================

pkt_02082F48 holds 0x02082F48, 0x02082F90, 0x02083028 and 0x020830C0, the byte-swapping memcpy family of the high window.  They
are hand-written ARM, not compiled C, and no C will ever reproduce them; the
finding is written up in full in docs/notes/pkt_02086368.c.txt section 1 by the
wave that first hit it.  The three tells, quoted there:

  * the byte tail at 0x0208314C is reached with a plain `b` (no relocation)
    from 0x02082F80, 0x02083018 and 0x020830B0 -- three functions jump into
    the middle of the body of a fourth, which no single translation unit can
    emit, since mwccarm gives every function its own section and a
    cross-section branch is a relocation;
  * a dispatcher at 0x02082EFC enters each variant's middle with
    `and r3, r1, #3; bic r1, r1, #3; cmp r3, #0; beq ...`;
  * the byte tail is `tst r2, #4` plus eight `ldrbne`/`strbne` pairs, a shape
    this front end emits for no loop.

They want the treatment layout/runtime_arm9.txt gives its routines: a curated
table entry, which would attest all eight at once.  Veredict for this lane:
blocking class `artifact`, evidence = the cross-section branch at 0x02082F80 ->
0x0208314C (plain `b`, no relocation field) and the 32-byte block loop's
`lsl`/`lsr` pair per word.  Not attempted as C on purpose: two waves of this
project have now spent budget on this family and both concluded it is not
compiler output.
