Bind a cell bank to an animation object, in one of two shapes chosen by the
last argument.  kind == 1 is the FLAT shape: one array of 0x5C-byte cells,
sized by func_02064648(a4), each initialised with func_0206428C against the
bank row func_020627D8(bank, 0) returns, and each given a `1` at +0x58.
Anything else is the TWO-LEVEL shape: func_020648E0(cells) counts 0x28-byte
group headers, the 0x58-byte cell array starts immediately after them at
`cells + n * 0x28`, each group header is opened with func_02064178(hdr, 1)
and made to point at its own cell, which is then initialised the same way.
Both arms end in a byte-identical four-call tail -- func_02062F38(o),
`o->f44 = a4`, func_02064140(&o->f48, 1), `o->f30 = 0` -- which the image
WRITES OUT TWICE, at 0x02064868..0x02064888 and again at
0x0206488C..0x020648AC.

Everything reproduces.  All ELEVEN relocation sites resolve (nine `label`,
one `sole-ref`, and func_020627D8 by `label x34`), the two duplicated tails
come out duplicated, both `(u16)(i + 1)` loop counters come out as the
`add/lsl #16/cmp .. lsr #16/lsr #16/bhi` quintet, and from 0x0206477C
onward the two columns are instruction-for-instruction identical, only
shifted by one slot and with the callee-saved set rotated.

    original                          compiled

cell sb->r4) through both arms.

The a4 respill is worth recording on its own.  The image reads the fifth
stack argument at 0x02064760 and stores it straight back to its OWN incoming
slot at 0x02064774, then re-reads it at 0x02064788 for the func_02064648

Only `void *volatile a4` plus an explicit self-assignment reproduces that
pattern; volatile alone gives the four reads and no write, and the
self-assignment alone gives the write but lets the value stay live in a
register across the branch.
